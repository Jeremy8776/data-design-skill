# Reporting protocol

Use this reference for the architect-authored report.

The report is the fourth stage of **Ingest -> Analyse -> Clarifying questions -> Report**. Do not use report writing to bypass unresolved consequential questions. If the architect chooses to proceed with one unresolved, label it as an open risk or explicit assumption.

## Reading order

1. Thesis in plain language.
2. The few decisions that materially change the architecture or operating model.
3. Confirmed evidence and human-certified answers.
4. Important unknowns and source coverage limits.
5. Recommendation, risks, next actions, and kill criteria.
6. Detailed source register and provenance.

The report should make sense before the reader reaches tables or counts. Use short sections, restrained density, and descriptive labels. Keep technical implementation detail behind the decision it supports.

The self-contained HTML report may add interaction without changing evidence: use the four-stage case spine for navigation, visualise relative source scale, reveal registers progressively, filter deterministic observations, and present one clarifying question at a time. Every interaction must remain keyboard accessible, functional without a network connection, and honest about whether it changes the portable case. Viewing, filtering, or copying a question does not certify an answer.

## Required distinctions

Label statements as appropriate:

- **Source fact:** returned directly by the authorised source route.
- **Deterministic observation:** a transparent pattern in source metadata or content.
- **Model proposal:** a hypothesis or question generated by an LLM.
- **Human verified:** an attributed organisational answer.
- **Architect conclusion:** the consultant's judgement and recommendation.
- **Unknown:** consequential evidence is missing or unavailable.

Do not convert a model proposal to human verified because it sounds plausible. Do not describe an unavailable capability as a platform defect when it may be a connector or scope limitation.

## Recommendation quality

Tie each recommendation to:

- the problem it solves;
- the evidence and answer supporting it;
- the smallest viable intervention;
- dependencies and permissions;
- an owner and next decision;
- a risk or kill criterion.

When the evidence does not support a recommendation, say what should be tested next rather than filling the gap with generic AI advice.
